Weeks 1 to 2

Listen, rank, fix

The ranking on this page is an outsider’s. Two weeks with sales and security make it Deepgram’s.

  • Read the stalled deals with sales, and the last ten security questionnaires.Which missing piece stopped which deal, and what it was worth.SalesSecurity
  • Fix every place where public pages disagree.Eight today. Each is a page edit with a named owner. No engineering.Docs and site

How I will know. A ranked list of blockers with deal value beside each, agreed with the head of sales.

Weeks 3 to 6

How I will know. Document requests handled by a person fall toward zero, and time in security review is measured for the first time.

Weeks 5 to 10

Close the access gaps

These are the questionnaire answers that are “no” today. Each ships in the Console with the product manager who owns it.

  • Multi-factor login an organization can enforce.The customer who asked in public needed it to pass an audit.Console
  • Service accounts.Keys that belong to the project. Today, removing a team member disables every key they created.ConsoleAPIs
  • An admin audit log.Who changed a role, made a key or signed in, exportable through the API.ConsoleAPIs
  • One switch for model training.Today, opting out is a flag on every request, or a call to Deepgram.ConsoleAPIs

How I will know. A buyer can answer the identity section of a questionnaire with yes, and point to where.

Weeks 8 to 12

Start the slow work

Federal readiness takes longer than twelve weeks. The job here is to start the clock on the right route.

  • A software bill of materials with every self-hosted release.Only one rival mentions one at all. Deepgram already ships FIPS-validated images, so this is a lead to extend.Self-hosted imagesSecurity
  • FedRAMP: a decision memo, not a promise.The 2026 rules add an entry tier that a SOC 2 Type II report qualifies for. The memo recommends a tier, a route and a budget.LeadershipSecurity
  • Say where FIPS applies.Validated images exist for self-hosted. State plainly what holds for the hosted API and for Dedicated.Docs and siteManaged endpoint

How I will know. Leadership decides on FedRAMP with real numbers, and self-hosted customers pull a bill of materials without asking.

What I would cut

  • Automated user provisioning.Only two rivals offer it. It follows single sign-on, not the other way round.
  • Custom roles.Three fixed roles answer most questionnaires. An auditor role is the first I would add.
  • ISO 27001.Four rivals claim it. It is an audit program that belongs to the security lead, not a product feature.
  • Full FedRAMP authorization.It takes longer than twelve weeks at any tier. The memo starts it.

What would change this plan

  • If most stalled deals are federal,the FedRAMP memo moves to week 3 and the access work waits.
  • If single sign-on is held back by quality, not staffing,its launch slips and the trust center goes first alone.
  • If security already has a trust center under way,I join that work instead of starting one.
  • If sales names a blocker that is not on this page,it goes to the top.

How I would measure it

Four numbers, reported every two weeks.

I can see no baseline for any of them from outside. Setting the baselines is week 1 work.

  • Days a deal spends in security review.The number this role exists to move.
  • Share of document requests served without a person.The trust center’s job.
  • Share of enterprise accounts on single sign-on with multi-factor login.Adoption, not launch.
  • Security questionnaires answered from the trust center alone.Each one is hours a sales engineer gets back.
